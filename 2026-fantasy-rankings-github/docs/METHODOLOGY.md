# Ranking methodology

## League settings

- Half-PPR scoring
- One quarterback
- Two running backs
- Three wide receivers
- One tight end
- One flex position

The three-wide-receiver lineup increases the relative value of wide receivers compared with common two-wide-receiver formats.

## Ranking process

Players are ranked using projected fantasy production, positional scarcity, expected role, depth-chart position, injury and roster status, and replacement value within the stated league format.

ADP is a market reference rather than the ranking formula. Differences between rank and ADP represent the author's evaluation of a player's value relative to the market.

## ADP treatment

The September 9 ADP combines available information from Yahoo, DraftKings, Underdog and NFC/NFFC sources. Yahoo wide-receiver ADP is normalized by positional tier to account for its two-wide-receiver default room before it is combined with sources that more closely resemble the three-wide-receiver format.

The `ADP Updated` field records when the market inputs were last refreshed. Historical snapshots retain the ADP that was available at publication time.

## Injury and roster changes

Material news may change a player's rank before ADP catches up. The date, old rank, new rank and reason should be recorded in `CHANGELOG.md`. A new dated snapshot should be created rather than changing an older snapshot.

## End-of-season evaluation

Evaluation should include:

- Overall and positional rank correlation with season results
- Mean absolute error in positional rank
- Top-12, top-24 and top-36 hit rates
- Performance relative to publication-date ADP
- Full-season and points-per-game results
- Separate identification of injuries, suspensions and role changes after publication
- Review of major omissions, successful fades and incorrect convictions

Both raw and availability-adjusted results should be published. Injuries should remain visible rather than being removed from the record.
